ScopeOut: Guided Deal Scoping

Scoping a software deal usually means a rep applying packaging rules from memory or a spreadsheet, and the mistakes show up later as a surprise re-scope, a discount fight, or an unhappy first renewal. I built ScopeOut to turn those rules into a guided discovery flow, so every rep scopes the same way and every decision shows its reason.

  • Sales engineering
  • Rules engine
  • Catalog-driven
  • Approval workflow
  • Vercel serverless
  • Vanilla JS
Try the live demo

The problem

  • Packaging rules live in a rep’s head or a pricing spreadsheet, so two reps can scope the same customer two different ways.
  • Discovery answers rarely make it into the CRM in a usable form, so the reasoning behind a deal is lost by the time it reaches deal desk or delivery.
  • Changing packaging means retraining every rep, and old rules linger for months.

The approach

  • Discovery first: the rep answers plain-language questions from the call, and the scope builds itself as they go.
  • Rules, not guesses: rules can only raise the requirement, so the strictest one sets the plan, and every decision is shown with its reason.
  • One catalog file per product: questions, plans, rules, price books and outputs all live in data. Import a new file and the tool reconfigures with no code changes.
  • Built on a proven engine: the same rules engine runs my chimney planner, which shows it isn’t tied to one industry.

For the sales team

  • Example scenarios load a realistic customer in one click, for training or for a live call.
  • A bill of scope, a plain-language summary, and a CRM-ready record come out of every session.
  • Discounts above a rep’s limit need a manager’s approval, and buyers never see a price that hasn’t been approved.
  • Buyers can get a branded email summary, sent by a small serverless function protected by a CAPTCHA and rate limits.

For RevOps

  • Packaging, price books, discount limits and simple rules are edited in a draft, then published to every rep at once.
  • A change log shows exactly what moved between versions, price by price.
  • Catalog imports are validated line by line, so a broken rule or a typo in a formula is caught before it reaches a rep.

At a glance

  • 1catalog file per product: questions, plans, rules and prices
  • 2industries on one engine: software deals and chimney systems
  • 3roles: rep, sales manager, RevOps admin

Using it on a sales call

  • Start with discovery, not a feature tour, and let the prospect’s answers decide what the scope needs.
  • Load a scenario that matches their situation, so they watch their own deal get scoped.
  • Switch to the buyer view to show what their own customers would see.
  • Send a branded summary at the end, so the scope and its reasoning travel with the deal.

What I’d highlight

  • Solutions engineering end to end. Defining the buyer’s problem, designing the rules model, and building the tool that solves it.
  • Configuration as data. Moving business rules out of code means the people who own the rules can change them.
  • AI-assisted development. Built quickly with an AI coding assistant, while I owned the product decisions, the rules, and the testing.

Try it yourself

  • Load an example scenario and watch the plan and the usage squares change as you edit answers.
  • Switch to Buyer to see the self-serve versions for executives and project leads.
  • On the Admin tab, choose the RevOps admin role, change a rule, and publish it.
  • Import a catalog on the Admin tab: try the clean catalog, then the one with planted errors to see validation catch them.
