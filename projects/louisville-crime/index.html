Louisville Crime: 2024 vs. 2025

Two-year comparison of 140,212 Louisville Metro Police Department (LMPD) incident reports (69,876 offenses in 2024 vs. 70,336 in 2025) paired with daily NOAA weather observations and lunar phases.

  • Tableau
  • Python
  • Public data
  • Correlation analysis
  • Year-over-year
  • 140,212reported offenses analyzed (69,876 in 2024 · 70,336 in 2025, +0.7% YoY)
  • r = 0.51daily high-temp correlation in both years (+0.73 to +0.76 offenses per °F)
  • -20.1%drop in auto thefts in 2025 (-1,161), alongside homicide (-26.1%) & robbery (-18.4%)
  • +28.5%surge in retail shoplifting (+912), with grocery store incidents up +73.6%
  • 88offenses on Jan 6, 2025 (winter storm & ice) — single lowest day across both years
  • -20.2%drop in Downtown (40202) offenses in 2025, while outer-ring ZIPs rose

01 · Temperature vs. Crime
Consistent · r = 0.51

Hot 85°F+ days drive +39% more daily crime than freezing days

NOAA daily high temperature explained ~26% of daily crime variance in both 2024 and 2025.
02 · Lunar & Rain Effect
Myth Debunked

Full moons (-5.9%) and rainy days (-4.7%) don’t spike crime

Across all 24 full moons in 2024–2025, full-moon days averaged 187.7/day vs. 191.9/day on other days.
03 · Offense & Venue Mix
Major Structural Shift

Auto theft & homicide plunged; retail shoplifting surged

Total volume was flat (+0.7%), but vehicle and violent crime fell while retail and drug reports rose.
04 · Seasonality & Day of Week
Mon → Fri/Sat Shift

Q3 summer peak held; busiest day shifted from Monday to weekend

July and August were #1 and #2 in both years, while 2025 volume shifted from Monday (-5.1%) to Friday/Saturday.
05 · Geography & ZIP Codes
Downtown -20.2%

Top 4 ZIPs held steady; Downtown dropped -20.2% while outer ring grew

The top 4 ZIPs kept 27% of all crime in the exact same order, while Downtown fell from #5 to #7.
06 · Daily Extremes & Lag
Jan 6 Blizzard: 88

Blizzard set a 2-year low (88); delayed reports spike the 1st of month

13.0% of offenses dated on the 1st were reported >7 days later (vs. 3.9% on other days), inflating 1st-of-month counts.
