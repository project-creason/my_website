Customer Management System for a Meal-Delivery Business

For the two-course IT Systems Project capstone in my M.S. in Applied Information Technology, our team analyzed the operations of a local meal-delivery company and built a customer management system around how it actually worked.

  • Systems analysis & design
  • BPMN process modeling
  • Database application
  • Technical documentation

What the business needed

  • Recurring meal-plan orders with start and end dates, quantities, and discounts.
  • Customer allergies and food aversions tracked and checked against orders, since a miss is a safety issue.
  • Vacation holds and multiple delivery locations, so each day's cooking and delivery list is right.

What we delivered

  • Business process models (BPMN swim lanes) of how the business ran, from creating menus to daily production and delivery.
  • A database application to manage customers, orders, meal plans and pricing, discounts, delivery locations, and vacation holds.
  • Allergies flagged separately from preferences, and carried through to the affected meal plans.
  • Operational reports: daily orders, deliveries by meal plan and location, allergies and aversions, vacations, customer list, and billing.
  • A step-by-step user's guide written for non-technical staff, including identity checks before account details are discussed.

Why it still matters

  • It's the same work I do today at enterprise scale: understand the business process first, model it, then build the system and the controls around it.
  • Data quality, safety-critical rules, and documentation people actually use were part of the design from day one.
